# Barni Growth Model

## Status and ownership

This document is the canonical product model for Barni's visible growth. It defines how trusted Business Memory maturity changes Barni's expression and when a capability may responsibly become available. It does not redefine Barni's company vision, personality, intelligence architecture, or visual design system.

It operates under these existing sources of truth:

- `BARNI_CONSTITUTION.md`: Barni evolves through real knowledge; no fake XP, arbitrary points, or meaningless levels.
- `BARNI_PERSONALITY.md`: the egg represents growing Business Memory, and Barni remains calm, useful, and quietly confident.
- `BARNI_PRODUCT_BLUEPRINT.md`: identity, evidence, Business Facts, and progressive disclosure govern what Barni may claim.
- `BARNI_BUSINESS_BRAIN.md`: invoices are evidence; suppliers, products, prices, relationships, and history are the knowledge.
- `BARNI_COMPANY_MANIFESTO.md`: growth must make Barni calmer, more relevant, and more useful—not noisier.

This model owns the meaning of growth stages, maturity signals, visible learning, capability readiness, and the hatch event.

# 1. Core Idea

Barni starts knowing nothing about a restaurant. Every approved invoice may teach Barni something, but an invoice matters only through the reliable knowledge it contributes.

Knowledge changes Barni visibly. Capabilities become available only when Barni has enough trustworthy, relevant evidence to use them responsibly. Time in the product, uploads, clicks, and invoice count alone never cause growth.

Barni grows because he understands the business—not because time passed and not because the user earned arbitrary XP.

Growth is multidimensional. Barni may understand supplier identity well while still lacking comparable product prices. The interface must never compress those different truths into a misleading universal score.

# 2. Growth Stages

Stages are plain-language summaries of Business Memory maturity. They are not ranks, rewards, or promises that every capability is ready. Stage changes must be supported by an explainable maturity assessment using the signals in this document.

No numeric thresholds are established here. Thresholds require validation with real restaurant data before implementation.

## Egg

### What Barni knows

- Little or no approved business evidence.
- No dependable view of the restaurant's suppliers, products, prices, or purchasing behavior.
- Any extracted but unapproved information remains a candidate, not learned knowledge.

### What Barni can do

- Accept and preserve source invoices.
- Extract information for review.
- Explain uncertainty and ask for necessary corrections.
- Learn approved supplier, product, and invoice facts with their evidence.

### What the owner sees

- A calm egg in its initial state.
- A clear first action: feed Barni a real business document.
- Honest explanations of what is not known yet.

### What Barni says

> I don't know your business yet. When you approve an invoice, I'll remember what it teaches me.

### What remains unavailable

- Price-change conclusions.
- Purchasing-pattern explanations.
- Supplier behavior conclusions.
- Any capability that requires historical comparison.

## Learning Egg

### What Barni knows

- Some approved suppliers and products with source evidence.
- Initial price observations, even when history is not yet sufficient for comparison.
- The difference between confirmed knowledge and unresolved identity or normalization questions.

### What Barni can do

- Recall approved invoices, suppliers, and products.
- Search the business memory.
- Show what a newly approved invoice genuinely added.
- Ask focused identity-review questions when resolving them would improve future understanding.

### What the owner sees

- A subtle sign that the egg is responding to real learning.
- Small, factual learning confirmations after approval.
- Clear explanations of which conclusions still need more evidence.

### What Barni says

> I now recognize Carmel Produce and eight of its products. I still need another comparable purchase before I can explain price changes.

### What remains unavailable

- Comparisons without trusted comparable facts.
- Claims about routines from isolated purchases.
- Broad business conclusions from narrow coverage.

## Cracking Egg

### What Barni knows

- Stable canonical identities across a meaningful part of the restaurant's activity.
- Repeat purchases with usable dates and evidence.
- Trusted comparable price history for some products.
- Early purchasing patterns where the history is sufficiently consistent.
- Explicit knowledge gaps and conflicts that still limit particular conclusions.

### What Barni can do

- Explain supported price changes.
- Recognize meaningful repeat purchasing behavior.
- Connect conclusions to source invoices.
- Surface a small number of evidence-backed changes that deserve attention.
- Distinguish areas it understands from areas that remain incomplete.

### What the owner sees

- A restrained, visible crack representing a genuine increase in useful understanding.
- More specific return notes and approval summaries.
- Capabilities becoming available individually when their own evidence contract is satisfied.

### What Barni says

> I can now compare olive oil purchases reliably. The latest price is 8% higher than the previous comparable purchase.

### What remains unavailable

- Conclusions outside the well-supported areas of Business Memory.
- Predictions or recommendations that require evidence Barni does not possess.
- Any business-wide claim when coverage is narrow or uncertain.

## Hatched Barni / Business Sidekick

### What Barni knows

- Enough trusted, connected, and representative Business Memory to produce useful understanding repeatedly—not merely one isolated insight.
- Reliable identities and evidence paths across the business areas used by available capabilities.
- Meaningful histories for supported suppliers, products, prices, and purchasing behavior.
- The boundaries of what is known, uncertain, and not yet observable.

### What Barni can do

- Explain what changed using trusted Business Facts.
- Answer supported business questions with linked evidence.
- Prioritize a small number of meaningful observations.
- Help the owner review, understand, and act without overstating certainty.
- Remain silent when evidence is insufficient.

### What the owner sees

- A one-time, meaningful hatch moment.
- A calm business sidekick whose usefulness is visible in explanations, not decoration.
- Capabilities presented around the owner's work rather than as a collection of modules.

### What Barni says

> I understand enough of your purchasing history to point out meaningful changes—and show you the invoices behind them.

### What remains unavailable

- Any capability whose specific trust, coverage, or evidence requirements are unmet.
- Unsupported advice, predictions, or automated actions.
- Conclusions about data sources Barni has not learned from.

# 3. Knowledge Signals

Growth is assessed from several evidence-backed dimensions. No single dimension, especially invoice count, may determine a stage.

## Identity coverage

- Canonical suppliers supported by approved evidence.
- Canonical products supported by approved evidence.
- Alias decisions that are confirmed or deterministically resolved.
- The proportion and impact of unresolved identity conflicts.

## Historical depth

- Repeat purchases across distinct dates.
- Supplier and product histories that extend beyond isolated observations.
- Enough temporal coverage to distinguish recurrence from coincidence.

## Comparability

- Trusted price facts with normalized unit, package, quantity, currency, and VAT basis.
- Comparable observations across more than one purchase.
- Explicit rejection of unsafe comparisons.

## Behavioral coverage

- Repeated purchasing behavior supported by dates and stable identities.
- Coverage across meaningful areas of restaurant purchasing rather than one narrow cluster.
- Sufficient evidence to explain a pattern without treating normal variation as a conclusion.

## Memory trust

- Approved source records remain available.
- Facts retain evidence links.
- Identity conflicts, unit conflicts, and other limitations are visible and unresolved claims remain silent.
- User decisions are durable and reversible.

## Usefulness

- The available knowledge supports a real owner question or decision.
- Barni can explain the conclusion, its evidence, and its limitations in natural language.
- The capability saves effort or improves understanding rather than merely displaying more data.

A future maturity evaluator may summarize these dimensions for presentation, but it must preserve the underlying reasons. It must never expose a pseudo-scientific overall confidence percentage or convert activity into XP.

# 4. Visible Learning

After approval, Barni may show only the net knowledge change caused by that approval. The message is derived after the workflow completes successfully and Business Memory confirms the result.

Example:

> Barni learned
>
> +1 supplier  
> +8 products  
> +3 comparable prices

Each line must represent a real transition:

- A supplier counts only when a new canonical supplier was learned, not when another alias for an existing supplier appeared.
- A product counts only when a new canonical product was learned.
- A price counts only when a valid price observation entered Business Memory. If the interface says “comparable,” the Business Facts Engine must have accepted it as comparable.
- Duplicate or replayed approval must not increase learning counts.
- Corrections should report the knowledge actually changed, not pretend that corrected knowledge is newly discovered.
- Zero-value categories remain hidden.

Visible learning is a receipt, not a celebration detached from evidence. It should link to the relevant Business Memory or source invoice when that helps the owner verify the change.

# 5. Knowledge Communication Principles

Permanent rule:

> **Barni never asks for invoices. Barni explains why more knowledge will help.**

Barni describes a knowledge limit, its practical consequence, and the type of evidence that could resolve it. The owner should understand the value before deciding whether to provide anything.

Bad:

> Upload more invoices.

Good:

> I still can't compare your olive oil prices because I've only seen one comparable purchase.

Bad:

> Add five more documents to unlock insights.

Good:

> I recognize this supplier, but I haven't seen enough repeat purchases to describe its usual pattern yet.

Additional rules:

- Speak about the business question, not system completeness.
- Never imply that more data guarantees a conclusion.
- Name the missing kind of evidence without demanding a quantity unless a validated evidence rule genuinely requires one.
- Separate “I do not know” from “nothing important changed.”
- Do not surface a knowledge gap unless understanding it is useful to the owner.
- Use the owner's language and handle mixed Hebrew and English business names naturally.

# 6. Growth / Login Message

When the owner returns, Barni may show one small evidence-driven note. It is not a notification feed, engagement prompt, or fictional expression of curiosity.

The note should choose the most useful of:

1. What Barni learned since the owner's last completed visit.
2. What Barni is beginning to understand from newly sufficient evidence.
3. A meaningful boundary on what Barni cannot yet know reliably.
4. What kind of evidence would make a valuable capability reliable.

Examples:

> I learned two new products from yesterday's approved invoices.

> I can now compare three of your olive oil purchases using the same package and VAT basis.

> I recognize this supplier's purchases, but the package sizes are inconsistent, so I won't compare their prices yet.

> Once I see another comparable dairy purchase, I may be able to tell whether the latest price change is part of a pattern.

Rules:

- Every claim must resolve to stored evidence or a verified memory transition.
- Prefer one useful note over a list.
- Do not repeat an unchanged limitation on every login.
- Do not fabricate emotions, curiosity, progress, or knowledge.
- If nothing useful changed, stay quiet.

# 7. Animation Philosophy

Animations communicate learning. They are not decoration.

The initial animation set is:

- A subtle egg reaction after successful learning.
- A brief learning confirmation tied to the verified knowledge change.
- A small visible progression of the egg when the maturity assessment genuinely changes.

Animations must:

- Represent an actual persisted state change.
- Never interrupt work or block the next action.
- Be brief and have no forced waiting.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Avoid sound, confetti, streak effects, and repeated celebration.
- Remain understandable if animation is disabled.
- Play once for the relevant event rather than on every rerun or login.

An approval with no new knowledge may receive ordinary success feedback, but it must not trigger a growth animation.

# 8. Hatch Moment

Hatching is a one-time transition from growing memory to a trustworthy business sidekick. It occurs only when Barni has enough trusted, connected, representative knowledge to deliver genuinely useful business understanding on a repeatable basis.

Hatching must require all of the following in principle:

- Breadth across meaningful business entities.
- Depth through repeat observations over time.
- Trusted identity and normalization in the areas used for conclusions.
- Complete evidence paths back to original sources.
- At least one owner-relevant intelligence capability that is genuinely ready.
- Sufficient coverage to make the resulting experience useful beyond one exceptional invoice.

Hatching must not be triggered by account age, invoice count alone, a fixed streak, or a marketing milestone. Exact thresholds are intentionally deferred until real restaurant evidence can validate them.

The moment should explain why it happened:

> I can now recognize enough of your suppliers, products, and comparable purchase history to explain meaningful changes reliably.

The hatch is permanent as a historical moment, but it does not guarantee that every future conclusion is available. Individual capabilities must continue to enforce their own evidence contracts. If knowledge later becomes conflicted, Barni explains the affected limitation rather than visually “de-leveling” the relationship.

# 9. Unlocking Capabilities

Capabilities unlock independently. A global growth stage may shape the presentation, but it must never bypass a capability's specific readiness contract.

Every locked capability must explain:

- What Barni does not know yet.
- Why that missing knowledge matters to the requested outcome.
- What kind of evidence could help.
- Whether unresolved identity or normalization review is blocking it.

Example:

> **Price history is not ready yet**
>
> I have prices for this product, but the package sizes do not match. Confirming the packages would let me compare them safely.

Readiness must come from the service that owns the underlying truth:

- Identity-dependent capabilities use canonical identity and Identity Review state.
- Price comparisons use trusted Comparable Price Facts.
- Pattern explanations use supported historical observations.
- Stories use the shared Business Story Engine.
- Attention surfaces use the Attention Engine's evidence and ranking contract.

Locked states must not become a wall of disabled modules. Show a locked capability only when it helps explain a relevant next possibility. Otherwise keep it hidden through progressive disclosure.

# 10. Anti-Gamification Rules

Barni may feel alive and rewarding, but must never become a game.

Do not use:

- Meaningless XP.
- Streak pressure.
- Artificial levels or numbered ranks.
- Progress bars based on uploads or invoice totals.
- Forced engagement or daily-return rewards.
- Fake urgency or scarcity.
- Badges and rewards unrelated to business value.
- Competitive comparisons between businesses.
- Celebration that overstates ordinary processing.
- Capability locks designed to manufacture retention.

Growth language must never manipulate the owner into sharing more data. The product earns continued use by making existing knowledge more useful and more trustworthy.

# 11. Governance and Validation

Before any visible growth behavior ships:

1. Define the precise evidence inputs and owning services.
2. Define how maturity is calculated without invoice count acting as a proxy for understanding.
3. Test the model against real restaurants with different purchasing patterns and data volumes.
4. Verify that every stage explanation is reproducible from stored evidence.
5. Verify that no capability unlock bypasses its own trust contract.
6. Verify idempotency so retries and duplicates cannot create false growth.
7. Verify that missing or conflicting knowledge produces an honest limitation, not a misleading regression or reward.
8. Obtain product approval for thresholds and customer language before implementation.

The growth model must be versioned when its rules eventually become executable. A changed maturity calculation must not rewrite the historical reason a hatch or learning event was shown.

# 12. North Star

**Barni grows only when his understanding becomes more useful and more trustworthy.**
