# Barni Product Vision

This document is the permanent product vision for Barni. It exists to help every developer, designer, product thinker, and future contributor make decisions that strengthen the same product promise.

Barni should grow in capability without losing its clarity, humanity, or trustworthiness.

# What Barni Is

Barni is an AI Business Memory and Operations Assistant for restaurants.

Every invoice teaches Barni something new about the business: its suppliers, products, prices, purchasing patterns, costs, and daily operations. Every interaction helps Barni understand that business better.

Barni transforms ordinary business activity into useful memory. Over time, that memory becomes context, supported insights, and practical guidance. The goal is not simply to store information. The goal is to help the restaurant remember what matters and act with confidence.

Barni should feel like an intelligent operations manager who learns quietly, remembers reliably, explains clearly, and helps at the right moment.

# What Barni Is Not

Barni is not invoice OCR software.

OCR is one way Barni learns, not the product itself. Extracting fields from an invoice has value only when those facts become reliable business memory.

Barni is not accounting software.

It does not exist to replace an accountant, bookkeeping system, or financial authority. It exists to help restaurant owners understand purchasing and operations more easily and make better-informed decisions from their own stored data.

Barni is also not a dashboard filled with numbers for their own sake. More information is not automatically more useful. Barni should surface what matters, explain why it matters, and stay quiet when the data does not support a conclusion.

# Mission

Barni exists to help restaurant owners:

- Save time.
- Save money.
- Make better decisions.

Restaurant owners are busy. They should not need to become analysts, accountants, or software experts to understand their business. Barni should turn complexity into calm, useful clarity.

# Product Philosophy

## Business memory before features

Every feature should strengthen Barni's ability to learn, remember, explain, or help the user act. A feature that adds activity without improving memory or decisions is probably unnecessary.

## Evidence before conclusions

Barni never invents an insight. Facts must come from stored business data. Trends require sufficient history. Recommendations must explain the evidence behind them. When the evidence is incomplete, Barni should say so plainly.

Trust is more valuable than appearing intelligent.

## Reliability before novelty

Restaurant operations depend on accurate records and predictable workflows. Barni must preserve existing data, protect auditability, handle uncertainty safely, and favor proven usefulness over impressive but fragile behavior.

## Simplicity before complexity

The best Barni experience makes a difficult business question feel easy. It should prefer one clear answer over five competing messages, one obvious action over several equal choices, and natural language over technical terminology.

## Explain before recommending

Barni should first show what happened and the evidence supporting it. Only then should it suggest a practical, conservative action. Recommendations must never overstate what the available data proves.

# The Personality of Barni

Barni is calm, capable, warm, intelligent, and human.

Barni speaks like a trusted operations manager—not a robot and not a salesperson. It is concise without being cold, confident without pretending certainty, and proactive without creating alarm.

Barni should:

- Remember important details reliably.
- Explain changes in natural language.
- Acknowledge uncertainty honestly.
- Suggest one useful next step when evidence supports it.
- Avoid technical language in the product experience.
- Never create urgency merely to attract attention.
- Make the restaurant manager feel informed and in control.

# Long-Term Vision

Barni becomes the living operational memory of a restaurant.

As the business teaches Barni through invoices and everyday interactions, Barni develops a clearer understanding of suppliers, products, pricing, recipes, costs, recurring decisions, and operational patterns.

Over time, Barni should help the restaurant:

- Find important information immediately.
- Notice meaningful changes before they become expensive problems.
- Prepare for supplier conversations with reliable evidence.
- Understand product and recipe costs more clearly.
- Reduce repetitive administrative work.
- Preserve operational knowledge that would otherwise live in memory, paper, or scattered files.
- Make decisions with greater speed and confidence.

The long-term goal is not to manage more invoices. It is to build a business that remembers—and an assistant that becomes more useful as that memory grows.

# User Experience Principles

Every screen should reduce stress and increase confidence.

Restaurant owners are busy, so Barni should always be simple, calm, and easy to understand. The experience must respect their time and attention.

Every screen should answer one primary question within five seconds.

To achieve that:

- Lead with the most important answer, not the underlying data structure.
- Use concise, natural language.
- Prefer clear summaries and cards before detailed tables.
- Make the primary action visually obvious.
- Use whitespace to create calm and hierarchy.
- Keep statuses, money, dates, and comparisons consistent.
- Make uncertainty visible without making it alarming.
- Use color as supporting context, never as the only signal.
- Keep detailed evidence available for trust and auditability.
- Remove anything that competes with the screen's primary purpose.

Every interaction should leave the user with a clearer understanding of what happened, what Barni knows, and what—if anything—deserves attention.

# Definition of Success

Barni succeeds when restaurant owners feel that it understands their business, remembers what matters, and quietly helps them run it better.

Success is not measured by the number of screens, features, alerts, or extracted fields. It is measured by meaningful outcomes:

- Less time spent finding and reviewing business information.
- Fewer avoidable purchasing mistakes.
- Clearer understanding of suppliers, products, prices, and costs.
- Greater confidence in operational decisions.
- Lower stress when dealing with invoices and purchasing history.
- Reliable business knowledge that improves with continued use.

A successful Barni experience should feel like hiring an intelligent operations manager, not buying another piece of software.

# Decision Filter

Before implementing any feature always ask:

1. Does it save time?
2. Does it save money?
3. Does it reduce stress?
4. Does it improve confidence?
5. Would a restaurant manager understand it within five seconds?

If the answer is "no" to all of these, the feature probably should not be built.
